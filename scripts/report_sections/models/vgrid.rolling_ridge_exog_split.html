How it works

The model works on the v-grid embedding: the prices of a curve read at 64 volumes evenly spaced from 0 to a fixed volume cap. The cap is a high percentile of the total volume of the training auctions, 99 for demand and residual and 99.9 for supply, so the same position always means the same volume in every auction.

  1. Query. For each of the 48 intervals, one ridge regression predicts the 64 numbers of the delivery day D from the 64 numbers of the same interval 7 days earlier, five calendar features (the day of week as a sine and a cosine, a weekend flag, and the month as a sine and a cosine), the market-state proxy and the weather source. The 20 principal components are taken from the 64 embedding numbers alone, and the calendar, proxy and weather features are standardised and passed on without compression. The ridge penalty is 200.
  2. Retrieval. The candidates are the real past curves of the same interval and the same weekday, dated on or before the monthly cut-off. The forecast is the candidate whose embedding is closest to the query in Euclidean distance, exactly as the market produced it.
  3. Refit. The model refits at the start of every test month on the auctions dated on or before the first day of that month minus 7 days. That data includes the earlier test months.

The market-state proxy holds thirteen daily numbers, all read from the day 7 days before the delivery day. Five describe that day's prices and volume: the mean, median and spread of the prices, the total volume, and the number of bid or offer rows. Two are read from that day's interp embeddings: the mean length of the 64-number vectors and their mean price range. The other six are trend numbers: the 7-day and 28-day trailing mean price, two price trends, a price z-score and a volume trend. The proxy also adds the day of year as a sine and a cosine, and Western Australian holiday flags.

The weather source is ten daily Perth values read from 7 days before the delivery day: the highest, lowest and mean temperature, the highest apparent temperature, rain, the highest and mean wind speed, solar radiation, humidity and surface pressure.

The curve of this model is also one of the two curves that the adaptive blends mix with the lag-7 curve.
