Demand - corrector.volume_shift_wfc

How it works

A demand curve gives, for one half-hour auction, how much energy the buyers bid at each price. The price falls as the volume grows, and every demand curve ends at the price floor of -1,000 $/MWh. The volume where a curve reaches the floor is its cliff volume.

From one week to the next, most of the change in a demand curve is a move of its cliff volume, and its shape changes much less. If the lag-7 curve is moved along the volume axis by the best amount for each auction, its HC-MAE on the test year falls from 148.97 to 34.69. That number uses the true curve, so it is a ceiling and not a forecast. This model forecasts the cliff volume and moves a typical recent curve to it.

  1. Shape. At each volume, the shape takes the median price of the 14 curves of the same interval from 7 to 20 days before the delivery day. It starts at the smallest of their 14 start volumes and ends at the median of their 14 end volumes.
  2. Cliff volume. A gradient-boosted tree model with the absolute error loss forecasts the cliff volume of the delivery day. It reads the cliff volumes and the end volumes of the same interval 7 to 34 days earlier, the daily mean cliff volumes of the same days, the calendar of the delivery day, the market-state proxy and weather_fc. It is refitted at the start of every test month on the auctions dated on or before the first day of that month minus 7 days.
  3. Shift. The shape moves along the volume axis by the forecast cliff volume minus its own cliff volume. Its start and end volumes do not change, so the forecast is never longer than the curves it is built from.

weather_fc is a simulated weather forecast: the measured Perth weather of the delivery day with random noise added. A real 7-day forecast is less accurate, so this score is an upper bound. On the validation year, the same volume model without weather features adds about 1 $/MWh over the median shape alone, which is within the noise.
