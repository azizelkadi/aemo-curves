Demand - medoids.hcmae_embedding

How it works

A medoid is a real training curve chosen by k-medoids to stand for a group of curves. Each curve is described by 48 numbers, its exact HC-MAE to each of 48 medoids, and the model predicts those 48 numbers.

The 48 medoids are real training curves chosen under HC-MAE, from the training window only (1 January 2021 to 30 April 2024). The search reads each curve at 2,048 equally spaced volumes, which gives a fast approximation of HC-MAE. It runs FasterPAM, a fast version of Partition Around Medoids, on a sample of 12,000 training curves spread evenly over the months and intervals. Then, on all training curves, each curve is assigned to its nearest medoid, and a medoid moves to another curve of its group when that lowers the summed distance to the curves of the group, for at most 6 rounds.

  1. Query. For each of the 48 intervals, one ridge regression predicts the 48 numbers of the delivery day D from the 48 numbers of the same interval 7 days earlier, five calendar features (the day of week as a sine and a cosine, a weekend flag, and the month as a sine and a cosine) and the market-state proxy. The features are standardised and reduced to 20 principal components, and the ridge penalty is 200.
  2. Candidates. The candidate set holds the curves of the same interval on any weekday, dated from 34 to 7 days before D.
  3. Median. The candidates whose 48 numbers are closest to the prediction in Euclidean distance are kept, 5 of them for demand and 10 for supply. At each volume, the forecast price is the median of their prices, so the forecast is a new step curve and not a medoid. It starts at the median of their start volumes and ends at the median of their end volumes.
  4. Refit. The model refits at the start of every test month on the auctions dated on or before the first day of that month minus 7 days. That data includes the earlier test months.

The market-state proxy holds thirteen daily numbers, all read from the day 7 days before the delivery day. Five describe that day's prices and volume: the mean, median and spread of the prices, the total volume, and the number of bid or offer rows. Two are read from that day's interp embeddings: the mean length of the 64-number vectors and their mean price range. The other six are trend numbers: the 7-day and 28-day trailing mean price, two price trends, a price z-score and a volume trend. The proxy also adds the day of year as a sine and a cosine, and Western Australian holiday flags.
